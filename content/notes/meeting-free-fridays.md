Sample note, replace me.

Deep work needs long, unbroken stretches of time. A calendar with a meeting every ninety minutes has no such stretches, however short the meetings are.[^maker] A day without meetings gives everyone at least one.

## How it works

Nobody schedules a recurring meeting on Friday. One-off meetings need a reason that cannot wait until Monday. Incidents are the obvious exception. Everything else moves into writing, which is why this habit depends on [[Docs as artefacts]].

## What goes wrong

1. **Erosion.** One "quick sync" is harmless. The fifth one means the rule is gone. Somebody has to say no, and it should be the manager.
2. **Thursday overload.** Meetings do not disappear, they pile up the day before. Cut some instead of moving them.
3. **Silence mistaken for absence.** People are working, not hiding. Agree on how to reach someone when it really matters.

## What you get

- Reviews that are read properly instead of skimmed between calls.
- The hard problem of the week finally gets four hours in a row.
- A natural moment to write the weekly note.

I have not yet written up how this interacts with regular one-to-one conversations; that will live in [[one-on-ones-that-matter]] when it exists.

---

Start with a trial month and ask the team afterwards. Few people ask for their meetings back.

[^maker]: The classic statement of this is Paul Graham's essay on the maker's schedule and the manager's schedule.
